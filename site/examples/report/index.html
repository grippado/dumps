An old phone can serve our public pages, provided we accept no guarantee of uptime

A spare Motorola phone, plugged in at home, has been serving our public pages through Cloudflare. Over five days it held up on memory, disk and recovery after a reboot. It also dropped off the internet twice on one evening, and we have no long-term availability figure.

Summary

  • Capacity is not the constraint. With every service running, about 4.6 GB of memory and 211 GB of disk remain free; the web stack uses well under 2% of memory.
  • Availability is the constraint, and it is unmeasured. Two short outages occurred on 19 November and cleared on their own. No continuous monitoring was in place, so we cannot state an uptime percentage.
  • Recommendation: approve for personal and low-stakes public pages. Do not use it for anything with a commitment to users until an uptime monitor, a heat and battery check and a fallback host are in place.

Scope and sources

This review covers one device serving the public site at dumps.gripp.link between its last reboot test on 14 November 2026 and readings taken on 19 November 2026. The administration area sits behind Cloudflare Access and was not assessed. Sources are direct readings on the device (memory, disk, process list, uptime), one controlled reboot, and observation of two incidents. Electricity use, temperature and battery health were not measured.

Findings

1. Two outages on one evening, both self-healing, cause not confirmed

Confidence: medium on impact, low on cause

On 19 November the tunnel dropped twice for a few minutes. During the first drop, remote access failed with a websocket error and the public site returned HTTP 502 and then 530 before recovering without intervention. At that moment the system load average read 11 on an 8-core processor. The load came from Android itself: the publishing service was at 0.3% CPU and the tunnel at 0.8%.

We therefore do not believe our software caused the outages, but we have not established what did. Android’s own background activity is the leading explanation and remains unproven.

2. The device recovers from a reboot without help

Confidence: high, one test

On 14 November the phone was restarted for real, not simulated. All three supervised services came back by themselves. The phone finished booting in about 45 seconds and the services were serving at about 95 seconds of uptime. A power cut or an Android update should therefore cost under two minutes of downtime, assuming the phone powers back on. This rests on a single test.

3. Memory and disk leave a wide margin

Confidence: high

The web stack is small. The three main processes together hold at most about 127 MB of memory, against about 4.6 GB still available with everything running. Disk is not a concern: 211 GB of 256 GB is free, and the Termux environment was cut from 764 MB to 183 MB during cleanup.

4. Five days of continuous uptime, but no availability record

Confidence: high on uptime, none on availability

At 21:21 on 19 November the device had been running for 5 days and 7 hours without a restart. Device uptime is not site availability: the two outages in Finding 1 happened inside that window. A planned one-hour monitoring run, checking services, battery and temperature every ten minutes, was deliberately skipped. We have no uptime percentage and no temperature data.

5. The cost is close to zero, but not fully counted

Confidence: medium

The phone was already owned and had been sitting in a box, so there was no purchase. Cloudflare provides the tunnel and access control. Electricity has not been measured.

Named risks

  • No service levelNobody guarantees this hardware, home connection or power supply. There is no one to call.
  • Single deviceOne phone, one location, one network. If it fails, the public pages are down until it is repaired or replaced.
  • Android background limitsAndroid is built for a phone in a pocket, not a server. It can throttle or pause background work and was the likely source of the load spike in Finding 1.
  • No monitoring yetOutages are noticed only when someone happens to look. Nothing alerts us today.
  • Heat and battery unknownThe battery has been on charge around the clock. Its temperature and condition have not been checked.

Recommendations

Do now

  1. Approve the phone for personal and low-stakes public pages only.Next step: record the decision; no change to the current setup.
  2. Add an external uptime monitor that checks the public site every few minutes and sends an alert on failure.Next step: configure a free external check against the public URL; review the first month of results.
  3. Run the skipped battery and temperature check.Next step: log battery level, temperature and service status every 10 minutes for at least one hour, ideally one day.

Consider later

  1. Prepare a fallback host so the static pages can move quickly if the phone is down for long.Next step: choose a static host and document the switch-over steps.
  2. Investigate the Android load spike and, where Android allows it, exempt Termux from battery optimisation.Next step: capture the process list the next time load rises.
  3. Revisit this decision after one month of monitoring data.Next step: re-review with a measured uptime percentage.

Appendix

Method
  • Memory, disk, process CPU and uptime were read on the device on 19 November 2026, with all services running.
  • The reboot test on 14 November 2026 was a full restart of the phone, timed from power-on to services answering.
  • Incident readings were taken while the outage was in progress; no automated log of start and end times exists.
  • Percentages in Table 3 are derived from the readings and rounded. Nothing in this report is estimated except where marked with “about” or “~”, which reflects the precision of the original reading.
What was not measured
  • Long-term availability (uptime percentage).
  • Device and battery temperature, battery health.
  • Electricity consumption and cost.
  • Response time and traffic volume for visitors.
  • The root cause of the 19 November outages.
Glossary
Cloudflare Tunnel
An outbound connection from the phone to Cloudflare. Visitors reach Cloudflare, which forwards them through the tunnel to the phone.
Cloudflare Access
A login gate in front of the administration area.
HTTP 502 and 530
Error codes returned by Cloudflare when it cannot reach the server behind it.
Load average
The average number of tasks waiting for the processor. On an 8-core device, a sustained value above 8 means work is queuing.
Termux and runit
A Linux environment that runs as an Android app, and the small supervisor inside it that restarts services if they stop.
